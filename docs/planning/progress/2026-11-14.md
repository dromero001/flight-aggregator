## Decisions I made (agent's call)

- **Two business models as types.** `Airline` is a sealed interface: a `NetworkCarrier` feeds one hub (hub ↔ every other hub, hub ↔ the spokes and long-haul airports it gateways, as before), and a `LowCostCarrier` flies point-to-point between every pair of its bases. `RouteNetwork` derives each airline's routes from its type in one exhaustive `switch`.
- **Hubs as in the brief:** IB and UX at MAD, BA at LHR, AF at CDG, LH at FRA, KL at AMS, TP at LIS, AZ at FCO, LX at ZRH.
- **Low-cost bases (real bases or destinations of each carrier):** Ryanair at LPA, MAN, EDI, EIN, BER, NAP, FAO, BOD, OPO, VCE and HAM. easyJet at CDG, AMS, LIS and GVA. Vueling at BCN, PMI and IBZ. They are mostly disjoint on purpose, because every shared base mixes airlines at that airport. SVQ and NCE stay outside every low-cost network, so SVQ-NCE still needs exactly two stops (`containsRoutesReachableOnlyWithTwoStops`). In reality Ryanair and Vueling base at SVQ and easyJet at NCE.
- **BCN has no network carrier any more:** with Vueling low-cost, BCN's links to the other hubs are flown by those hubs' carriers.
- **Low-cost routes fly up to 5 flights a day, network routes up to 2** (`operatingProbability` 0.42 for both). This frequency gap is the lever that reaches 40%: see the first item below.

## Needs the user's review

- **Where the 40% comes from.** A carrier that feeds a single hub can never fly a 2-stop itinerary on its own without passing through its hub twice, which the new rule forbids. Two-stop itineraries are ~90% of the volume, because a 24 h layover connects to almost every departure of the next day. So single-airline 2-stops can only come from point-to-point networks, and the largest one dominates (chains grow with about the fourth power of its number of bases). Result: **95% of the single-airline itineraries are Ryanair's**, and each network carrier has 1–3k (its direct flights and its 1-stops through its hub). Filtering by IB, LH or BA still returns thousands of itineraries, but the "40%" is mostly Ryanair. Alternatives, not taken because each is a design decision:
  - (a) Network carriers with secondary hubs or domestic point-to-point routes (e.g. TP at LIS + OPO, LX at ZRH + GVA). The brief gives one hub each.
  - (b) Measure the target over itineraries with at most one stop.
  - (c) Add secondary airports that only low-cost carriers serve, to spread the volume across several low-cost carriers.
  - (d) Accept the current mix.
- **Frequency and Ryanair's weight.** In the spike, with the same daily maximum for both models, the share stayed at 7–32% depending on the base sets. The defaults give low-cost routes twice the network routes' average frequency (3 vs 1.5 scheduled flights a day). As a result, Ryanair operates 45% of the distinct legs (3,896 of 8,755), and its bases see 9–17 departures a day, as many as the hubs (LHR 13, FRA 15, MAD 24). Ryanair is Europe's largest airline, but per route, real network carriers fly their hub-to-hub routes more often than low-cost carriers fly theirs.
- **Calibration noise.** `FlightSchedule` draws every route's flights from one sequential seeded stream. Reordering the same routes moved the volume by 6% (cycle 4r), and a throwaway spike with other seeds moved the share by about ±3 points. The tests pin the default seed and clock, so they are deterministic. Deriving each route's draws from its own key, as `SeededRandom`'s contract describes, would remove the order dependence. Not done: it is outside this brief.

### Decisions I made (agent's call, round 2)

- **"Route to or from a hub" means a hub at either end, whatever the airline.** So all of easyJet's routes count as hub routes (three of its four bases are hubs), and so do Vueling's Barcelona routes and the long-haul routes. Only Ryanair's routes and Vueling's PMI-IBZ are non-hub routes. The airline's business model no longer affects frequency, only which routes it flies.
- **Values 4 / 2 / 0.345.** Non-hub routes keep the maximum the network routes had (2), so the low-cost double frequency is gone, and hub routes get twice that. Of the configurations swept, it was the one closest to 1M and to 40%.
- **Route identity = airline + origin + destination,** both for the per-route seed and for the flight-numbering order.
- **One thing per commit:** cycle 12 kept the old frequencies (2/5) with a small retune, so the frequency rule and the recalibration are separate commits.

Reducing it further means fewer bases, or not connecting every pair of bases: a network design decision, not taken.

### Rulings and their reasons

- **google-java-format 1.36.1, not 1.37.0.** 1.37.0 turned `Style` into a record and Spotless 8.10.4 cannot load it. Spotless 8.11.0 was 12 days old, which §6.1 excludes. Cost: one minor version behind on the formatter.
- **Spring is allowed in `application`.** The application layer uses Spring annotations (retry, concurrency limit), so the rule allows `org.springframework..` there and still forbids it in `domain`. A meta-test pins this.
- **`javax..` and `jdk..` types are flagged in the domain; `java..` is allowed.** The domain needs neither. The non-mutation-proven clean-adapters test was accepted because its violation test covers the rule.
- **Coverage: 99.1% lines (347/350), 92.9% branches (65/70)** against gates of 90% and 80%. `**/config/**` is excluded.
- **CI concurrency.** Superseded runs are cancelled only off main, and the job is capped at 30 minutes, so every push to main keeps its result; intermediate commits rely on the local build required by `CLAUDE.md`. The re-review found one edge (a pending main run replaced by a newer pending one), so main runs now have a group per commit: `ci-${{ github.ref == 'refs/heads/main' && github.sha || github.ref }}`.
- **PR #1 as the throwaway red check.** CI triggers on push only for main, so a branch push alone started no run. A draft PR "ci-red-check (throwaway, never merge)" triggered the `pull_request` event with a failing assertion, then was closed unmerged. The failing commit never reached main.

Pending, for the user:

- **`./gradlew clean build` could fail in the formatter** (resolved in `29d4383`; was seen once by the code reviewer, not caused by the earlier waves). Spotless adds a `doLast` to every project's `clean` task that clears its formatter cache and closes the cached class loaders (`SpotlessCache.clearOnce`, registered in `SpotlessPlugin.apply`). On Gradle 9 the "clear once" guard does not hold, because `rootProject` is a per-project wrapper, so every `clean` clears. With `clean` and `build` in one invocation, one module's `clean` closed the loader another module's Spotless task was using, and the `package-info`-only modules failed with `NoClassDefFoundError`. Upstream: [diffplug/spotless#3067](https://github.com/diffplug/spotless/issues/3067) (open). Workaround, in `flightaggregator.java-conventions.gradle.kts`: every `SpotlessTask` `mustRunAfter` the `clean` path of every project (path strings, so the configuration cache still works); remove it once a Spotless release that fixes the issue passes the 14-day rule. Measured in a warm daemon with `--no-build-cache`: before, 5 failures in 6 `clean build` runs; after, 0 in 10, plus 0 in 2 from a fresh daemon, and a second plain `./gradlew build` reuses the configuration cache. Without `--no-build-cache` the race did not show (6 of 6 green), because the cache restores the Spotless tasks instead of running them. Note from the upstream thread: a failed Spotless lint can be stored by the build cache, after which `./gradlew build` keeps failing until the cache is cleared; today's cache is clean (plain `./gradlew build` green).
- **What the hook does not cover:** commands split over several lines no longer bypass it (a backslash continuation or a line break is joined before matching, with and without `jq`; closed in this follow-up). Still open, low risk: aliases and shell variables (`G=git; $G push --force`); quoted refs such as `git push origin ":main"`; quotes inside `-c` values; `checkout ./`; and forms outside the review's list, such as `git checkout HEAD -- .`, `checkout -f`, `switch --discard-changes` and `stash drop`/`clear`. GitHub's ruleset refuses force pushes to `main` and its deletion anyway, so the remaining gaps can lose local work at worst. The hook still blocks harmless commands that merely quote a destructive one, for example in a commit message (risk R10, fails safe; workaround `git commit -F <file>`).
